The worker's persistence service: it checkpoints every authoritative entity carrying a [`PersistentEntity`](/docs/reference/persistence/persistent-entity) into an [`IPersistenceStore`](/docs/reference/persistence/i-persistence-store), and brings those entities back when this worker gains the lease of a container they were saved in. Owned by [`NebulaWorker`](/docs/reference/worker/nebula-worker) and reached as `worker.Persistence`; null when persistence is off.

It is never on the per-tick path. Checkpoints run from the worker's `Update`, at most [`MaxSavesPerFrame`](#maxsavesperframe) of them per frame, and a store that is unreachable only means the saves are retried later: the mesh keeps simulating either way.

Restores are deliberately timid, because a persisted entity coming back must never become a second copy of one that is still alive somewhere. A freshly leased container waits [`NebulaConfig.PersistenceRestoreGraceSeconds`](/docs/reference/core/nebula-config#persistencerestoregraceseconds) so the previous owner's handover wins; a record whose key is already alive here is skipped; a record written moments ago by a worker that is still alive waits another grace period, in case that worker is about to hand the entity over; and if a handover arrives after a restore anyway, the incoming entity wins and the restored duplicate is despawned *without* deleting the record.

Restores are also bounded in how hard they ask. A worker can gain hundreds of containers at once (a re-deal after another worker died, a rebalance, a burst of runtime chunks), and each frame the containers that are due are read in batches of at most [`MaxContainersPerRestoreLoad`](#maxcontainersperrestoreload) through [`IPersistenceStore.LoadContainers`](/docs/reference/persistence/i-persistence-store#loadcontainers), with at most [`MaxRestoreLoadsInFlight`](#maxrestoreloadsinflight) of those batches waiting on the store at a time. The rest wait for a later frame. Nothing about a lease change can make the worker send an unbounded burst of requests to the orchestrator, which serves the control plane's heartbeats from the same address.

What rode in a carrier comes back with the carrier, and those reads are batched the same way. A carrier that spawns with authority is marked due; each frame the due carriers are read together through [`IPersistenceStore.LoadCarried`](/docs/reference/persistence/i-persistence-store#loadcarried), at most [`MaxCarriersPerRestoreLoad`](#maxcarriersperrestoreload) per read and at most [`MaxRestoreLoadsInFlight`](#maxrestoreloadsinflight) of those reads waiting at a time, so a restore that brings back a hangar full of vehicles costs one read rather than one per vehicle.

### ContainerRestored [#containerrestored]

```csharp
public event Action<string, int> ContainerRestored;
```

Every persisted record of a container this worker has just gained has been read and dealt with: the container id, and how many entities were actually brought back (0 when there was nothing saved). Raised once per lease, on the main thread, after the grace period and the load. This is the seam the scope lifecycle waits on before a restored scope admits clients (`docs/scope-lifecycle.md`); NEB-242 exposes it to games as `OnContainerRestored`.

### CheckpointContainer [#checkpointcontainer]

```csharp
public int CheckpointContainer(string containerId);
```

Save every authoritative persistent entity in `containerId` right now, whatever the checkpoint schedule says, and return how many were saved. The forced checkpoint of the retire sequence: the saves are issued here and [`IPersistenceStore.WhenWritten`](/docs/reference/persistence/i-persistence-store#whenwritten) is the barrier that says they reached the store. Does not despawn anything. An entity riding in a vehicle in the container, at any depth, is in it too: emptying the container takes it ([`NebulaWorker.EmptyContainer`](/docs/reference/worker/nebula-worker#emptycontainer)), so it is saved here with everything else, aboard its carrier.

### Apply [#apply]

```csharp
public void Apply(PersistedEntityRecord record, NetworkIdentity identity, bool applyPose = true);
```

Put a record's state back onto `identity`. Works both before the entity is spawned (the restore path) and on an entity this worker already owns, which is what a rejoining player's pawn wants: the game spawns the pawn, asks for the record with [`Load`](#load) and applies it when the answer arrives. Pass `applyPose` false to keep the entity where it is and take only its state. A spawned entity also keeps its current pose when this process has not registered the record's container or carrier, such as an unloaded chunk, because the saved position is relative to that container.
