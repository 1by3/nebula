Deal by load: every container (baked or runtime) costs what [`CostWeights`](/docs/reference/orchestrator/cost-weights) says its occupants cost, containers are ordered along a Morton curve of their centres so neighbours sit next to each other, and the curve is cut into one contiguous run of roughly equal cost per worker. Each run goes to the worker that already holds most of it, so a rebalance moves containers at run edges and nothing else. Nothing moves at all while the most loaded worker is within [`Threshold`](#threshold) of the average and every container has an owner; an orphan is then placed next to its neighbours' owner without disturbing anyone.

### MinGain [#mingain]

```csharp
public float MinGain = 0.1f;
```

The smallest improvement in the busiest worker's measured utilization that justifies moving containers during play (0.1 = a tenth of a tick budget). Checked only when [`AssignmentInput.Utilization`](/docs/reference/orchestrator/assignment-input#utilization) carries real measurements and every container already has an owner; an orphan is always placed.

### MinGainFloor [#mingainfloor]

```csharp
public float MinGainFloor = 0.35f;
```

How busy the busiest worker must already be before the [`MinGain`](#mingain) veto applies at all (a fraction of a tick budget; half the default scale-out line). Below it the utilization signal is too small to show a gain of [`MinGain`](#mingain) however lopsided the mesh is - a 3x cost imbalance over two nearly idle workers still measures as a few hundredths of a tick - and vetoing on it would freeze the layout until something is already hot. The cost-unit balance rule decides instead.

### MaxGroupUtilization [#maxgrouputilization]


A group (cohesion or affinity) whose containers cost more than one worker's tick budget is kept whole anyway and reported. This is that budget, as a fraction of a tick: 1 is "a whole worker". Raise it for a mesh that runs its workers past their tick budget on purpose; lower it to be told earlier.
